# Style DNA

## One Sentence

Clean, restrained, publication-ready scientific schematic: precise enough for researchers, readable in seconds.

## Required

- White or transparent-looking background.
- Single panel by default; 2-3 panels only when the source needs separation.
- Thin, clean schematic linework; vector-like clarity.
- Restrained palette: black/gray structure, one main accent color, one secondary color.
- Short labels only: usually 1-4 words per label.
- Clear visual hierarchy: main process/entity first, supporting context second.
- One image explains one mechanism, workflow, comparison, or conceptual summary.
- Use exact terminology supplied by the user.

## Research Review Figure Style

Use when the user wants a polished journal-style conceptual illustration, similar to Nature, Cell, or other research-review figures. This is a cross-domain style for biology, chemistry, physics, materials science, engineering, computation, and related fields.

- Combine a high-level context view with 1-3 zoomed mechanism or process panels when useful.
- Use clean white background, soft muted base colors, thin vector-like linework, and restrained accent colors.
- Prefer simplified schematic forms over realism: components should be recognizable but not literal unless source material requires it.
- Organize information into clear panels with small panel labels when multiple mechanisms, scales, or cases are shown.
- Use arrows, connectors, callouts, brackets, dotted guide lines, and inset panels to show relationships across scale or abstraction.
- Put short labels close to the relevant structure, process, or interaction.
- Use small outcome or effect callout boxes only when the source supports the claims. Keep outcome callouts text-only or directly tied to the mechanism; avoid icon tiles or decorative badge cards, especially for improvement claims, because they can read as pitch-deck graphics or imply unsupported measured results.
- Keep the figure dense enough for expert readers but structured enough to scan quickly.
- Show mechanism, flow, comparison, or consequence; avoid decorative science imagery.
- Do not invent entities, pathways, equations, materials, instruments, scales, conditions, outcomes, or quantitative evidence.

## Color Guidance

- Black/dark gray: outlines, arrows, labels, neutral structures.
- Blue/teal: primary components, systems, materials, fields, or flows.
- Orange: flow, intervention, perturbation, or transformation.
- Red/magenta: warning, inhibition, damage, failure, loss, or negative result.
- Green: activation, recovery, improvement, success, or desired outcome.

Do not make rainbow figures unless the user supplies a semantic color scheme.

## Avoid

- Stock science art, glossy 3D render style, sci-fi interfaces, neon gradients.
- Fake journal figures, fake datasets, fake axes, fake p-values, fake microscopy.
- Dense textbook plates or long explanatory paragraphs.
- Decorative domain motifs or equipment unrelated to the source.
- Photo-realistic objects, instruments, samples, materials, structures, or environments unless exact references are supplied.
- Long title text inside the figure.

## Typography

- Use a plain journal-style sans-serif for all labels: Helvetica by default; Arial if Helvetica is unavailable.
- Keep text regular weight unless a panel label or key entity needs emphasis.
- Use small unobtrusive panel labels only for multi-panel figures.
- Avoid decorative, condensed, handwritten, serif, monospace, sci-fi, or presentation-style fonts.
- For image prompts, ask for Helvetica/Arial-like readable sans-serif labels when exact font control is unavailable.

## Label Rules

- Keep labels legible and horizontal when possible.
- Prefer generic labels like "stimulus", "response", "sample", "sequencing", "model", or "prediction" when the source lacks exact names.
- Do not invent named entities, variables, materials, instruments, equations, scales, conditions, timepoints, pathways, mechanisms, or outcomes.
