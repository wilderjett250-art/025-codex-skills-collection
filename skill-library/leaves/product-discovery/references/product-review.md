# Review an existing product

Start with supplied brief, real screens, behavior, research and constraints. Describe one core user's task from entry to useful result, including failure/recovery. Separate observed facts from plausible assumptions.

Assess only decision-relevant dimensions:

- Value: which real pain or desired outcome is served, why the current workaround is inadequate, and what credible differentiation exists?
- Usability: can users discover the action, understand state and recover without losing work? Aesthetic preference is not usability evidence.
- Viability: ongoing support/operation cost, incentives, business fit and any applicable compliance constraints. Do not invent demand or revenue.
- Feasibility: integration/data dependencies and the hardest uncertainty. Refer a consequential technical decision to the architecture owner only when that work begins.

For each material finding, give evidence, affected user/result, smallest useful change and validation method. Prioritize must-fix failures separately from experiments and optional enhancements. Include what to preserve or remove; more features is not automatically better.

For uncertain claims, choose a cheap, falsifiable check with an observable outcome. Interviews, tracking changes and experiments affecting users need their established authorization. Preserve existing canonical product documents; a review does not require a new PRD or a mandatory approval ceremony.
